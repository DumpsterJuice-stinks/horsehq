# Kuphaldt Chapter 10 — Discrete Control Elements (Study Notes)

Companion to Ch 9. Where Ch 9 covers discrete **sensing** (inputs), Ch 10 covers
discrete **actuation** (outputs).

Full text: `Kuphaldt_Ch10_text.txt` · PDF: `Kuphaldt_Ch10_Discrete_Control_Elements.pdf`

## Core definition

> "A **discrete control element** is one that has but a **limited number of
> states (usually two: on and off)**. In the case of valves, this means a valve
> designed to operate either in 'open' mode or 'closed' mode, **not in-between**."

That last clause is the discrete/analog line again: an on/off valve versus a
throttling (analog) control valve.

## 10.1 On/off valves

- "An on/off valve is the **fluid equivalent of an electrical switch**: a device
  that either allows unimpeded flow or acts to prevent flow altogether."
- Uses: routing process fluid, **starting and stopping batch processes**, and
  engaging automated **safety (shutdown)** functions.
- Common styles: **ball, plug, butterfly (disk), gate, globe**.
- Ball, plug, and gate valves give a **nearly unimpeded path** when full-open.
- **Plug vs. ball:** a plug valve uses a **truncated cone** rather than a
  spherical ball, with a slot through the cone for fluid. The conical shape lets
  it **wedge tightly closed** for exceptional sealing.

## 10.3 Solenoid valves

- "A **solenoid** is nothing more than a **coil of wire designed to produce a
  magnetic field when energized**." The actuator attracts a **movable ferrous
  armature** into the coil's center, and that force actuates the valve mechanism.
- Classified by **number of ports ("ways")**:
  - **2-way** — flow into one port, out another. Analogous to a **single-pole
    single-throw (SPST)** electrical switch: one path for flow.
  - **3-way** — directs flow to one path or another, "much like a **single-pole
    double-throw (SPDT)** electrical switch."
- **Like electrical switches, solenoid valve symbols are always drawn in their
  "normal" (resting) state**, where the **return spring's** action determines
  the valve position.

## 10.4 On/off electric motor control circuits

- Motors act as discrete control elements when driving pumps, conveyor belts, or
  other transport machinery.
- "Of all the available electric motor types, the most common found in industrial
  applications (**by far**) is the **three-phase AC induction motor**."
- **Principle:** out-of-phase AC currents energize **stator coils/windings**
  arranged around a circle, producing a magnetic field that **appears to rotate**.
  Kuphaldt's analogy: **chaser lights** blinking in sequence — the bulbs are
  stationary, but the pattern appears to move.

## 10.5 Review of fundamental principles

- **"Normal" switch status** — resting condition, minimum stimulus.
- **"Seal-in" circuit** — "when an electrical relay uses **one of its own switch
  contacts to continue its own coil energization** after the initial triggering
  event has passed. Relevant to **motor starter circuits**."
- **Lenz's Law** — any magnetic field arising from electromagnetic induction
  **opposes the inducing field**. Relevant to AC induction motor operation.
- **Rotating magnetic field** — necessary to make an AC induction motor spin in a
  particular direction; generated by **polyphase field poles**. All AC induction
  motors need a polyphase field to **start** in a direction, "although a
  **single-phase** magnetic field is sufficient to **maintain** rotation once
  started."
