What to Look for in a Stud: A Buyer's Guide to Choosing a Stallion

The Horse HQ Team · August 2026

A stallion profile usually gives you three different kinds of information — pedigree, progeny record, and the horse's own lifetime performance — and they don't all answer the same question. Knowing which one to weigh most heavily depends entirely on what you're actually breeding for.

Pedigree Tells You What's Possible

Pedigree is potential, not proof. A strong bloodline behind a stallion — proven producers a generation or two back, a sire with a track record of passing down speed or cow sense or a certain build — tells you the genetic raw material is there. It doesn't tell you whether this specific stallion actually passes those traits on consistently. That's what the next section is for.

Progeny Earnings Are the Closest Thing to Proof

If a stallion has offspring on the ground already, their combined performance record — earnings, ROM, world show placings, whatever's relevant to your discipline — is the best evidence you'll get of what he actually produces, as opposed to what he's bred to produce. A first-season sire won't have this yet, which isn't disqualifying, but it does mean you're buying more on pedigree and less on proof. Know which one you're doing.

Lifetime Earnings Tell You About Him, Not His Foals

A stallion's own performance record is a real credential, but it's easy to overweight. Plenty of exceptional producers were average performers themselves, and plenty of standout competitors throw foals that never match them. Treat his own record as one data point among several — useful for judging soundness, mind, and conformation under pressure, less useful as a direct predictor of what he'll pass on.

Match the Cross, Not Just the Stallion

The best stallion on paper can still be the wrong cross for your specific mare. Look at what he's already produced out of mares similar to yours in type, and don't be afraid to email the farm and ask directly — most standing operations, including the ones on The Horse HQ's Member Stallions section, are set up for exactly that kind of pedigree compatibility conversation before you book.

Ask About Cover Terms Early

Stud fee is only part of the cost. Cover contract terms, live foal guarantees, chute fees, mare care while she's on-site, and shipped semen availability and viability all affect the real total cost of the breeding — and they vary a lot between farms. Get this in writing before you're emotionally committed to a particular cross.

The Short Version

  • Pedigree — what's genetically possible
  • Progeny earnings — what he's actually proven to produce
  • Lifetime earnings — what kind of individual he is, not a guarantee about his foals

Weigh all three against what you're actually trying to produce, not just which number looks most impressive on the page.
